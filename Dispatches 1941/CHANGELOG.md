# Dispatches 1941 changelog

Player-facing and rules-facing changes only (what a player or a balance check would notice). Newest first.
Add an entry in the same commit as the change. Engine changes live in packages/engine/CHANGELOG.md.
Format: version or date, then Fixed / Changed / Added. A change that moves the UI baseline must also be
listed in tests/baseline/known-diffs.json (see docs/WORKFLOW.md).

## Unreleased (2026-10)
### Changed
- **Shortages change the odds.** A meter below -4 takes points of probability off the best outcome of the contested decisions that put that meter at stake (not battles, which weigh their own shortages), and each point owed adds half a point more, up to 12. The decision says so in red before you choose ("Strain: Pipeline short, so the odds are 7 points worse for it"), and the roll uses the same weights. A decision is about the meter its check names, else the one its outcomes move most.
- **A meter at the floor keeps its debt.** What a cost takes below -10 is owed (up to 3 points, kept in the save), and later gains pay it before they raise the meter, so ten points of debt and one good decision no longer reads -9.
- **The end screen shows a command rank** (Private to General, out of 100) built from five things the player can see: how the war ended, the condition the command was left in, the battles (graded by how the plan held up), the decisions against the historical ones, and the objectives. Being removed from command holds it at Lieutenant, and Easy cannot reach General. The older style label ("The Fireman") stays beside it.
- **The end screen carries the theater map** with a slider to review the war report by report.
- **Five contested rolls that ignored the state of the war now respond to a meter** (the Moscow channel, the embargo, the early vote, the looser intelligence standard, the Tarawa hearing), as the other 36 already did. Data nodes can name a `rollMeter`.
- One map hint was keyed to a node that does not exist (`overlandChina44`) and now names `theMainlandCrisis44`.
- **Difficulty is chosen in the war room**: Easy, Normal or Hard, each with a plain summary. The title page has one button, Take Command, and no longer carries the long notes on the hard modes. Normal is now called Standard Issue Command; the hard modes keep their names.
- **The meters work as they do in 1940**: the bars are green when up and red when down and slide to the new reading, and each meter opens into three readings (Readiness: Training, Forces, Morale; Pipeline: Fuel & Oil, Shipping, Industry; Initiative: Intelligence, Command, Tempo) with a marker on any that moved since the last decision. The panel is on the report page, the outcome page and the end screen. The line explaining plus and minus is gone.
- **Advisers argue positions; they no longer speak.** All 337 adviser lines were invented first-person speech ("I would rather keep that air group intact..."). Each is now a third-person position, shown as "Adm. Nagano argues: ...". The four lines that are real quotations (Onishi proposing suicide attacks, Curtin turning to America, MacArthur on arriving in Australia, Groves on radiation) are shown in speech marks and logged with a source in `claims/quotations.json`; `npm run check-quotations` fails on any invented speech. The six doctrine cards state a position too, and the Yamamoto line in the war room is now his remark to Konoe, not a paraphrase in speech marks.
- **About 600 passages rewritten**: speculative reports now say so in their first sentence, and the commentary about the game itself ("the likelier outcome, and the one history's own…") is gone.
- **The briefs and introductions are one plain sentence or two**, with the editorialising removed.
- **No em dashes in the on-screen text.** `npm run check-writing` fails on a new one (see `../docs/WRITING.md`).

### Fixed
- **Map labels are readable on every zone.** The ink sat on the dark red of a Japanese-held zone and could not be read; labels now have a cream halo and are drawn after all the zones, so a neighbouring fill cannot cover one (Guangdong was half under Hunan-Guangxi). The map also had two elements with the same id when two maps were open; and it now has a text summary for a screen reader.
- **Two facts in the Guadalcanal battle** (found while checking the new map dates): in November 1942 Kusaka commanded the 11th Air Fleet at Rabaul (the Southeast Area Fleet was formed in December), and Rabaul is about 650 miles from Guadalcanal, not 560.
- **A hovered choice no longer goes black on black.** The fallback stylesheet for arbitrary-value classes was unlayered, so it beat Tailwind's hover colours (and its `sm:` sizes). It now sits in the `utilities` layer.
- Thirteen ending classifications for titles no war can return are gone, and `check-reachability` now fails on a leftover.
- **Facts corrected after a read of every report against the standard histories** (sources and what is still unchecked are in `claims/facts-round1.json`). Among them: Doolittle Raid casualties; Bataan prisoner numbers and the POW death rates; the Hull Note date and the Proposal B that preceded it; the Kido Butai sailed on 26 November, before the Imperial Conference; Kokoda, Ke-Go, Attu, Savo Island and Tarawa details; the Wake, Arcadia, Port Chicago and Indianapolis details; Lockwood's torpedo tests came in 1942 in Australia; MacArthur left Corregidor by PT boat and B-17; Compton did not sign the Franck Report (the adviser is now Franck); Parshall and Tully do not argue that Midway turned on minutes (the claim is gone); the bat-bomb node is dated May 1943.
- **Advisers and titles match the month.** Yamamoto no longer advises in 1944, Sugiyama not in 1947 to 1950; Togo, Terauchi, Hull, Hurley and Grew are shown under the post they held in that month (`ADVISOR_TITLE_BY_DATE`).
- **Fourteen ending titles that no war could produce are gone** from the endings list (11 of them were listed in the gallery as endings to find): the war always went on to a later decision whose own title came first. Removed: The Line Not Moved, Two Empires Two Different Wars, The War That Waited, A Different Kind of Ready, The Check Removed Before It Was Needed, One Hundred Million Together, Two Hundred Roots One Hour, The Line Nobody Had to Cross, Spent Regardless of the Reason, The Stalemate That Finally Moved, Everything But the Declaration, Europe First Meant Literally, Mercenaries in Everything But Name, Manila Left Behind.

### Added
- **A first-run guide** on the first report of a war ("How to read a report": the choices, the advisers, the three bars and what a low one costs, contested odds, battles, the map, save and home), open until "Got it" is pressed and then never again. It is also on the title page under How to read these reports.
- **`npm run check-a11y`** plays four seeded wars in jsdom and fails on a control with no accessible name, a duplicated id, a skipped heading level, an image with no alt, a screen change that leaves focus on the page, and a palette pair under its WCAG ratio. The UI baseline is now 64 runs (eight seeds a case).
- **China, the Dutch East Indies and the Philippines are broken down on the theater map.** China is eight zones (Manchukuo, North, East and South China, Hunan-Guangxi, Free China, Yan'an and the far west), the Indies five (Sumatra, Java, Borneo, Celebes, the Moluccas and Timor) and the Philippines three (Luzon, the Visayas, Mindanao). The zone borders are straight lines cut from the country outlines by `tools/split-pacific-zones.mjs`, so they are schematic. Each zone changes hands on its own day.
- **The map is dated by the day, not the year.** It shows the state on the day a report opens (it showed the end of that year, which gave away the year's outcomes), the slider steps back month by month, and the end screen's map and review slider use the same dates. The dates and what is unchecked are in `claims/map-dates-round1.json`; `npm run check-map` now checks the timeline, the year-end table and the readability of every report date.
- **The Order of Battle**, ported from 1940: three Pacific actions are fought on their own screen. The player commits a pool of effort across four arms, picks a commander and one of two approaches, buys intelligence on the enemy's setup (or lets the staff plan), makes a field decision, and reads the battle as dispatches before the roll. Midway (CINCPAC, the three carriers), the November battles off Guadalcanal (IGHQ, the bombardment and the convoy) and the Philippine Sea (CINCPAC, the pursuit). The plan moves the odds of the choice that hosts the battle (at most 30 points) and costs the meters (at most one a meter, two in all); the next report says how the battle was fought. Choices that open a battle are marked "Leads to battle planning". Each arm has an order-of-battle sheet with the units and what happened. A battle can be put down and picked up (Save and leave the field), and the plan is saved when it is committed. The hard modes put orders from above on each battle. The end screen counts the battles in the command rank and lists them; the title page has a Battle Record. Facts are in `claims/battles-round1.json`.
- `npm run check-battle-balance` (the allocation math and every battle's content, over every enemy setup, pool size and strand profile), `npm run check-undefined` (type-checks the assembled App.jsx and fails on a name that is used and never declared) and `npm run test:battle-resume` (saves a battle in its planning screen and again in its report, resumes both in a fresh page, and damages the save).
- **A glossary of 72 terms.** In a report, the outcome page and the epilogue, the first mention of each term is underlined with dots, and tapping it gives a note; the whole list is under Glossary on the title page. `npm run check-glossary` fails on a duplicate, a dead entry or a passage with too many underlines.
- **"What This Game Leaves Out"** on the title page: Nanjing, Unit 731, the forced brothels, the prisoners and internees, and what the Allies did, in plain terms and kept outside the decisions.
- `npm run check-rolls` (every contested roll must respond to a meter), `npm run check-map` (the map data: regions, geometry, years, statuses, overrides and node hints), and in `check-endings` the ceiling of each command and the spread of ranks.
- **Easy mode** (Shinano Command for IGHQ, Mark 14 Command for CINCPAC): every choice shows what it will do to the meters, the choice the record made is marked, and rewind is available. Not in the demo.
- `npm run test:logic` checks the readings and the modes. The UI baseline now has 48 runs (an Easy run and a staff-plan run for each campaign).
- **Audit suite** (`npm run audit`, mostly in `test:fast`), ported from 1940 and built on the game's own rules (`tools/audit-lib.mjs`): `check:orphans` now also proves every ending title can be produced; `check-reachability` (gallery entries that no campaign can return, flags written but never read, choices blocked at every meter state); `check-advisor-dates` (an adviser shown after death, removal or capture, before taking a post, or under a title that was not true that month, against the reviewed table `tests/adviser-tenures.json`); `check-outcome-sign` (unhedged triumph or disaster prose beside an impact that points the other way); `check-endings` (no ending takes more than 85% of random wars). Findings that are understood and not yet fixed are listed in `tests/audit-allowlist.json`: 15 flags that are written and never read, to be fixed in the fact-check pass.
- Save migrations and node aliases (`NODE_ALIASES`, `SAVE_MIGRATIONS`, `migrateSave`): an update can now upgrade saves instead of wiping them (docs/SAVES.md).
- Twenty Allied Pacific nodes now live in `src/data/alliedPacific.nodes.json`; text and choices are unchanged.
- `check:orphans` (reachability of every listed node) in the fast tests.
- Save-compatibility test (npm run test:saves) with committed old saves for both campaigns.
