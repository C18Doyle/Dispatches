# Dispatches 1914 changelog

Player-facing and rules-facing changes only (what a player or a balance check would notice). Newest first.
Add an entry in the same commit as the change. Engine changes live in packages/engine/CHANGELOG.md.
Format: version or date, then Fixed / Changed / Added. A change that moves the UI baseline must also be
listed in tests/baseline/known-diffs.json (see docs/WORKFLOW.md).

## 1.4.2 (2026-10-23)
### Added: the sketch of the ground and the day in bulletins, and the conditions of a report
- **The sketch of the ground.** An Order of Battle is planned on a sketch: one numbered sector for each of the four arms, the enemy above and our own side below, and the line between them. A counter is placed by pressing a sector and taken back by pressing the counter; the buttons under each arm still do the same for a keyboard or a screen reader.
- **The second day in bulletins.** The day is read as a morning, a noon and an evening bulletin for each arm, each marked gaining, holding or breaking, with the sketch redrawn for each hour so that the line moves forward, stays or falls back sector by sector. The morning opens the second day, before the order is given; the noon and the evening come with the after-action report, read off the order given and how the battle ended, so that they never disagree with the result. `battleStages` in src/61-battle.jsx does the reading, and `smoke.js` proves every battle gives every arm a status at every hour.
- **The conditions of a report.** Under the title: how old the news is, the weather the month usually brings in the command's main theatre, the state of the railways (the rail reading of the munitions meter) and, on the three reports where the figure is documented (the Somme, Operation Michael and the third Ypres), the guns that were fired, with the source named in src/48-conditions.jsx. Advisers who are trusted or silent are named. The age of news and the weather are modelled for the command and the period, not recorded for the day, and the table says so.
- **Signals and the press** are chosen when a file is set up. Signals in clear bring the news about thirty per cent sooner and move three points of weight on every contested order from its best outcome to its worst (the enemy overhears; battles read their own odds). A censored press prints no newspaper front pages.
- **Hard mode: allies.** Eight orders that an ally would object to (deferring the French share of the Somme, declining the British under Nivelle, keeping the British command independent of Foch, refusing the French an attack at Naroch, leaving Ukraine to the Germans, refusing Vienna's request for divisions, and others) say who must be brought round and cost a point of standing in Hard mode only.
- **The staff's beliefs name the evidence they rest on** (the General Staff's appreciation, French aerial reconnaissance, the Evidenzbureau, patrol and prisoner reports and the like).
- **Not done:** a ledger of casualties by date and the first-hand rail timetables, because there is no series of figures by date that this register can check; the rail reading stands in for the second.

## 1.4.0 (2026-10-19)
### Added: a free demo, a full download and a battle rig. Changed: the menu, the modes, the newspapers
- **The release is split into a free demo and the full game**, as in 1940 and 1941. `npm run build` makes the full game (dist/, and dist/full for the zip), the demo (dist/demo) and the battle rig (dist/rig). The demo is the French command, its first six orders (the covering of the frontier, Lorraine, the retreat, the sacked generals, the Marne as an Order of Battle and the race to the sea), standard mode only, and it ends on a screen that links to the full game; the other five commands are listed as locked. `itch.json` is set to push the demo to `dispatches-1914:web-demo`, the full game to `dispatches-1914:download-full` and to the restricted page `dispatches-1914-confidential:web-full`. `tests/demo.test.mjs` plays the demo to its end screen.
- **The battle rig.** `npm run rig` builds and serves a version of the game with a Battle rig entry on the menu: all fifteen Orders of Battle in a list, each opened at its own report with the enemy's setup, the strain on the meters (low, level or high) and Easy mode chosen. A battle runs to its result and goes back to the list, and nothing of it is saved. The full game has no such entry. `tests/rig.test.mjs` fights one through.
- **The menu.** The music switch is gone from every screen and lives in Settings only. The cards give a name and the campaign's opening date, with no date under the quotation.
- **The modes.** The three files are described in short bullets under the choice, and Easy is marked in a muted green and Hard in a muted red.
- **The newspapers.** The word Speculative no longer heads a front page. The page is set as the paper would set it: the masthead, a dateline with the weekday in the paper's own language for the French, Italian and German papers, the headline, a line saying it is from the paper's correspondent, justified columns in a serif and an aged page. A line at the foot says it is a page from the file, not from the record.

## 1.3.4 (2026-10-18)
### Changed: the text speaks from inside the war room, the interface says less, the soundtrack is lighter, and two reading aids
- **The live voice.** A report, an outcome, a bulletin, an event or a newspaper no longer looks back or forward from the day it describes. About forty sentences were rewritten or cut: the historians and what they argue, "afterwards", "will remember", "as it was always going to", "no one will ever know whether", "the war goes into 1919", and similar. Situations keep their length. The briefings' "Your seat" now says who sits in the seat and what stands under it on the first day, and no longer names their successors. `check-live-voice.js` holds the line (part of `validate`): it flags those phrases anywhere outside the epilogue, the dossiers, the battle after-action line and the notes that only the validators read, and it caps a menu quotation at 24 words, a seat at 75 and the note under an order at 26. Three outcomes that fell below the length floor had a sentence added from what the report already says, and one new fact (Joffre's order of the day at the Marne) is logged as a drafted claim.
- **Less interface text.** Six explanatory notes on the Settings screen are gone or cut to one short line, and so are the line about the staff's reading being right three times in four, the war record's battle note and a few more. `check-ui-copy.js` keeps a note or subtitle under seventeen words.
- **The soundtrack** is re-encoded from 4.1 MB to 1.7 MB (80 kbps), so the build is about 2.4 MB smaller. A Music on or off switch sits at the top left of every screen, and the music pauses when the tab is hidden.
- **Reading aids.** A Read aloud button on each report speaks its title, document and situation with the browser's own voice (it appears only where the browser has one). Settings has a Dyslexia-friendly typeface: a wider-spaced sans-serif that uses OpenDyslexic or Lexend if they are installed.

## 1.3.2 (2026-10-16)
### Changed: the front, the war record, the report and the second day of a battle
- **The menu.** The quotations carry the speaker's name under them and a date in small type, and the word "attributed" and the phrase "as he recalled it" are gone. The title line is credited to David Lloyd George, 1916, which is the common attribution and not a proven one (the earliest citation found is a book of 1932; the claims register marks it disputed). The Ottoman card now quotes Enver Pasha on letting the Goeben and Breslau through the Straits, as Djemal Pasha recorded it in his memoirs. The STAVKA seal is set smaller so that it fits on one line.
- **The war record.** The line about the per cent of the record that is open is gone, as is the "Not yet fought" line under a battle. The glossary lists each term with a capital for every word, and eleven everyday entries (blockade, corps, tank and the like) are gone. Every dossier can be read from the start, and each says in how many of the orders it argued for you followed it; the count is kept in the record.
- **The briefing.** "Your seat" now says what force the command has on the first day, in place of naming the first decision. The easy and hard files carry the name of a good and a bad piece of that army's equipment (Big Bertha and the Pickelhaube, the Soixante-Quinze and the Red Trousers, the Ilya Muromets and the Shared Rifle, the Lee-Enfield and the Mark I, the Skoda Mortar and the Horse-Drawn Gun, the Nusret and the Peabody-Martini), and battle planning is an approval stamp. A new screen opens at the top of the page. The French command's will meter is called Morale.
- **The report.** The map comes straight under the title. The Show background drop-down is gone, with the map's footnote about today's borders, the staff's note about grades and checks left, and the special-event line about meters. A contested order's result no longer waits in a sealed cable: there is nothing to break. The Meanwhile list shows the three nearest reports of the other commands, each with a sentence of what that headquarters faced.
- **The newspapers.** A speculative front page has a masthead, a dateline, a headline, a Speculative stamp and two columns, and the "Special event" label is gone.
- **Orders.** Twenty-seven orders that no adviser argues for carry one plain sentence under the label that says what the order would do (src/47-order-notes.jsx, proved by check-order-notes.js).
- **The soundtrack.** The theme from Dispatches 1940 (assets/theme.mp3) plays on a loop, on by default at half volume, once the player has tapped or pressed a key (no browser starts sound before that). Settings has Music, On or Off, with a volume slider while it is on. The track is bundled into the page as a data URL so that a copy opened from disk plays it too, and the build is about 5 MB larger.
- **The second day of a battle.** In place of moving counters between arms, the player gives one of a few orders from the field: hold the line, press the attack, fall back to the second line, or send the reserve to an arm that is short.

## 1.3.1 (2026-10-15)
### Changed: a plainer front of the game, from the first round of testing
- **The menu.** The line under the title is now: this war, like the next war, is a war to end war. The "File No. 1914" row is gone from the top of every screen. Each command is a card with its nation, a real quotation from the first days of the war and its starting date, and nothing that says what the command will be asked: the Kaiser (attributed) on being home before the leaves fall, Poincare on the sacred union, the Tsar's manifesto, Grey on the lamps, Franz Joseph in To My Peoples, and the Ottoman note of neutrality of 8 August 1914. The number of decisions and the end date are gone. How to play is no longer on the menu: it is in Settings, and its text is a good deal shorter. The War record and Settings are titles only.
- **The briefing.** The "What you will be asked" section, the explanation of the three meters and the How to play button are gone; the first decision is the last line of "Your seat". Standard mode and the battle-planning box carry no explanation, and Easy and Hard have a line each.
- **The report.** The "The decision" line with its skip-to-orders link is gone. The "Show background" summary and the battle summaries no longer use an arrow that some phones draw as an emoji; no symbol that can draw as one is left in the game, and the meter carets are drawn in CSS.
- **After an order.** The outcome no longer says "a modelled alternative" or "as it happened", no longer offers the historical record and no longer lists the changes to the strands of each meter. The sealed cable has no picture of an envelope and nothing that moves.
- **Signing.** A tap signs, by default; holding is a setting (a new key, so everyone starts on a tap).
- **The staff's checks are a debate.** A check no longer says that a belief was confirmed or wrong. The staff is divided, and the reports lean toward it or against it; an order that rests on a belief says the staff now doubts it or has come to trust it.
- **The map.** Paper is the default theme (anyone who has not chosen a theme gets it), and the four themes have more distinct colours for the Central Powers, the Entente, neutrals and the sea. The empire is the Central Powers' from the first day on the Ottoman map, as its provinces already were. The Meanwhile list sits directly under the map in the expanded view, and says 1 day and not 1 days.
- **The war record on a phone.** The per-command cards in the Service tab were using the menu card's layout and squeezed their text into a column one letter wide; they have their own layout.
- **A document at the head of eight reports.** Where the game would have invented a wire bulletin, a real text is quoted and named: Holtzendorff's memorandum on unrestricted submarine warfare, the first German note to President Wilson, Petain on waiting for the tanks and the Americans, the Tsar's manifesto of 2 August, Kitchener's message to the troops, Haig's order of 11 April 1918, Franz Joseph's To My Peoples and the Balfour Declaration. Each passage is logged as a web-checked quotation, and `check-documents.js` proves it. The other reports keep their bulletins.
- **The fact-check.** Another 55 claims in the register are checked against a source, from 380 to 435 of 838, with their sources in `claims/sources.json`; the rest are still marked as drafted. Three statements were wrong or loose and are corrected: Holtzendorff promised five months, not six; the courts-martial of 1917 carried out between 26 and 49 death sentences by different counts, not "a few dozen"; and Lyautey left the war ministry after a quarrel in the Chamber, not by refusing to sign the plan.
- **Wording.** Fifteen sentences of the form "X is not A. It is B" were rewritten as plain statements, and the sentences that closed each of the new endings with a moral were replaced by statements of what happens.

## 1.3.0 (2026-10-14)
### Added: more endings, orders that can fail, the ground against the record, the file at its close, newspaper specials, reading settings
- **Twenty-four more endings**, four for each command, taking the total from 47 to 71 (13 each for the German, French, Russian and British commands, 10 for the Austro-Hungarian and 9 for the Ottoman). Each is earned by a combination of departures from the record carried to the last reports (a neutral mediator in the chair, an ally that breaks first, a pursuit to the Rhine, an army that was never asked to die, the tanks in mass, an independent monarchy, an empire that stayed out, and others), has a rank tier and a ground rating, and ends with what actually happened. A rule needs a flag that the record does not set, so a file that follows the record never meets one. Two rules are read before the command's own route and guard against a collapse by their own meters, so that they can be reached at all.
- **Forty-one more orders can fail.** An ambitious order that the record did not give (an assault, a transfer of divisions, a refusal of a superior) is now a contested one, with a second branch, written for it, in which it fails; the outcome it had is the branch that works. The Germans have 14 contested orders, the French 16, the Russians 16, the British 14, the Austro-Hungarians 13 and the Ottomans 17. Each cites the dispute in the histories that it stands for (`DISPUTES` in `src/44-risks.jsx`). `check-risks.js` keeps the weights, impacts and texts sound.
- **The ground against the record.** Seventy-five places, a dozen or so for each command, are marked on the map when the file's orders took them or gave them up against the record: a ring and a plus for ground held or taken beyond it, a square and a cross for ground lost or not taken. The marks are listed with the expanded map, as speculative, and they move the ground part of the command rank by up to four points either way. `check-ground.js` proves each mark is read from a flag that an order of that command sets.
- **How your file went**, on the ending screen after the rank: the front on the first day and on the last, side by side, with the ground marked on the last; the orders that left the record, in order; and the staff the file ended with (trusted, gone quiet, relieved), the beliefs checked and the battles planned.
- **Newspaper specials.** Thirty-seven special events, six or seven for each command, are front pages of papers in other countries (The Times, Le Temps, the Berliner Tageblatt, Novoye Vremya, the Corriere della Sera, the New York Times and others) that say what a speculative order did to somebody else's war. Each is shown once, on its own screen with a masthead, in the window after the order, and begins "Speculative."
- **Reading settings.** Line spacing (standard, roomy, wide), line length (standard, narrow) and motion (follow the device, or reduce it, which stops the marching front line, the stamp, the reports sliding in and the dials moving whatever the system says), and an Easy reading button that sets plain type, roomy spacing, narrow lines and no motion together.
- **Two wording corrections**: the Turnip Winter is remembered as one of the hardest of the war and not the worst; a sentence in the Stavka plans report that read badly after the earlier wording pass is mended.

### Also in 1.3.0: 2026-10, depth
### Added: the staff's reading, the advisers' trust, two-day battles, orders on the map; three more Ottoman reports
- **The staff's reading** (fog of war). Twelve reports across the six commands list what the staff believes (the French staff that the German right is weak, the Russian front staff that the Eighth Army is falling back, the Ottoman staff that the British will attack in the east), each with the grade the staff gave it, which is no guide. A belief can be checked, three times in a file (two in hard mode), and says whether it holds and what the truth was. The orders that rest on a belief say so; the odds dial of a rolled order resting on a false belief shows better odds than the roll has, until it is checked; an order that does not rest on a belief found false earns a point of will. Easy mode shows every verdict and has no fog.
- **The advisers' trust.** An order that follows an adviser's argument earns that adviser a point of trust (up to +3) and each adviser passed over loses half a point (to -3). At -2 the adviser goes quiet and the argument is not shown; at -3 the adviser asks to be relieved, by a special event written from the dossier; a trusted adviser (+3) brings one more check on a belief.
- **Battles have a second day.** After the plan is issued, a field report names the enemy that was really met (the intelligence summary is right three times in four) and says how the first day went. Up to two counters may then be moved between arms, each worth three times what it would have been in the dark, and a stance is chosen: hold the plan, press the attack (a third more of the plan's edge or error, and a point of manpower) or steady the line (half of it). The staff's plan held with nothing moved still plays the record's odds.
- **Orders on the map.** A hundred and ten reports have their orders drawn on the report's map: a solid arrow for an attack, a dashed one for a move, a dashed arrow with an open head for a withdrawal and a ring for a stand. The order the pointer or the keyboard is on is drawn in the accent and the others lightly; the order given is drawn on its outcome and the last three are drawn faintly on the next reports.
- **Three more Ottoman reports** (Aqaba, flour and firewood for Medina, the ceasefire at Erzincan), 21 decisions in all, and a recap line for every Ottoman report from Romani on.
- **The Stavka record path**: the Emperor's assuming the command is now an 80 to 20 roll (it was 60 to 40), so a file that gives every order the record gave reaches the settled ending about 80 per cent of the time and not 60.
- **CI runs the games side by side** (one job per game, one for the cross-game tests and one for the browser steps), so a pull request waits for the slowest game and not for the sum.
- **Fact-check pass on the drafted claims.** About forty drafted claims were read against web sources and moved to web-checked (the armistice terms and Erzberger's delegation, Brest-Litovsk and the German advance of February 1918, Czernin's term, the Military Service Act, the first convoy, the Gallipoli evacuations, the Bulgarian treaty, the Tsar's assumption of command, Malmaison and others), with the sources added to claims/sources.json. Where a source disagreed the text was corrected: the armistice gave fifteen days for the evacuation of the west (not fourteen) and granted several concessions (not one); the Military Service Act took effect on 2 March (not 17 February); Rupprecht's objection to the devastation of the ground was to the Empire's prestige and the troops' discipline; the Hindenburg Programme is dated to the end of August; the Somme no-ground order is logged as disputed (Falkenhayn or Second Army). Claims about positions and modelled alternatives stay drafted, because they cannot be checked against a source.
- **The command rank is mostly territory and power now.** Seven parts out of 100: the ground held at the close (30, a rating of 0 to 3 given to every ending: the heartland invaded, a large part of the territory lost, the homeland held with losses, all its own ground held or recovered), the power left (20, the three meters together), nothing run dry (10), the staff kept (10: a base of 6, two points for each of two trusted advisers, and a loss for each adviser who has gone quiet or been relieved), the ending's tier (10, down from 30), restraint (10) and the mode (10, easy 4, standard 7, hard 10). The Field Marshal rank now starts at 82 (it was 80). One tier changed: the earlier negotiated peace for the French command is 2, not 3, because it leaves the occupied departments in German hands. `check-rank.js` now also keeps every ending's ground and checks that ground and power move the score more than the tier does.
- **Wording**: twenty-two uses of "in the record" in the campaign text were varied.

### Also in 1.3.0: 2026-10, flow
### Changed: the front of the game, one calendar, and a second wave of depth
- **The menu says which command is which.** The six commands are listed by side (the Central Powers, the Entente), each card led by its nation (Germany, France, Russia, Britain, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire), then the command's English name, its native name and seat, a line on what it asks of you, its dates and the number of decisions. How to play, the War record and Settings are three buttons under the commands.
- **A briefing before the war room.** Choosing a command opens a briefing: where things stand, your seat, what you will be asked, the map on the first day, what the three meters mean for that command, and then the set-up of the file: Easy, Standard or Hard mode, and whether to take control of battle planning. Both moved off the menu. A button enters the war room.
- **How to play** replaces "What this game leaves out" and Feedback on the menu: what you are doing, reading a report, orders and risk, battles, modes, endings, dates, and what the game leaves out as its last section. The feedback option and the author's note on the ending screen are gone.
- **Settings is a screen.** Text size is a slider with three stops (Standard, Larger, Largest) and a live sample; colours are swatches; type, signing and sound are grouped; the calendar is stated; one button returns every setting to its default.
- **One calendar.** Every date is written as in the UK (4 August 1914) and reckoned in the Western calendar. The Russian command's reports are converted from Old Style on screen, and its dates in the war record and the map. `check-briefings.js` fails a date written the American way or with an ordinal.
- **A report reads in order**: the decision, the wire, the situation, the map, the background, the meters, the orders.
- **Special events.** Thirteen events (the Turnip Winter, the April strikes of 1917, the casualty lists of 22 August, a new government in Paris, the shell shortage, the roads behind the front, Zeppelins over London, the Rising in Dublin, the death of the Emperor, the January strikes, the gaps in the railway, typhus in the army and others) come between two reports when the state of the file calls for them and the date is right. They are not decisions and change no meter; each is shown once.
- **A prelude for every command**, dated in the days before its first report: the Kaiser's question (1 August), ten kilometres on the French frontier (30 July), two offensives (Russia, 1 August), four divisions or six (5 August), mobilising against Russia (30 July) and the offer from Berlin (29 July). Each has three orders, and one is a roll.
- **Deeper forks.** Three-way decisions rise from 32 to 52 and contested orders from 34 to 46. Eleven more decisions have a third order (the Aisne, the Meuse reserves, Masuria, Przemysl, Naroch, Helles, conscription, the Oise, the Kolubara, the Hungarian divisions, Koprukoy); three new Ottoman reports (Romani, Baghdad, Gaza) take that command from 14 decisions to 18.
- **Four more Orders of Battle**: Tannenberg (Russian), the Chemin des Dames (French), Flers and Courcelette (British) and Ypres in November (German), 15 in all. 58 more claims, 12 of them for events.

### Also in 1.3.0: 2026-10, forks
### Added: many more forks, two more battles, a clearer report and outcome, a shareable file
- **More three-way decisions and more rolls.** Decisions with three orders rise from 3 to 32 and contested orders from 17 to 34. Each command gained a third, speculative order at several decisions, six of them leading into a short detour of their own (the German timetable in the east, the French week of the frontier battles, the Russian march on Lemberg, the British plateau at Passchendaele, the Austro-Hungarian flank in Galicia, the Ottoman clothing for the winter at Sarikamis) that rejoins the record. A detour begins "Speculative." and an order in it that returns to the record is the historical one. New flags have readers further on, the endings' recaps list the new decisions, and 54 more claims are in the fact-check.

- **A few gates were retuned** so that an order other than the record's is not shut out by a meter alone (Verdun, Malmaison, the Marne, Lodz, Chantilly, Romania, Gallipoli, Erzurum). The record's own order is never gated.
- **Each report states its decision.** A line under the title says what is to be decided, with a link that jumps to the orders; the first letter of the report is the drop cap, not the title block. All 131 decisions have a line (`check-asks.js`).

- **Share this file.** A closed file shows a card (command, ending and its badge, rank, orders given, contested, battles planned, departures from the record and the first three) that can be copied as text, saved as a picture (drawn in the browser, never uploaded) or printed.
- **Tools.** `npm run playthrough` plays every command to an ending in a real browser, three ways each (the record in easy mode, two seeded random ones with a battle planned by hand and by the staff, in different themes), with an accessibility check on the way; it runs in CI. `node tools/balance.mjs` plays 4,000 files by six styles of player and writes docs/BALANCE.md; the findings are in docs/BALANCE_NOTES.md.

### Also in 1.3.0: 2026-10, polish
### Added: the Sepia look, colour themes, a phone pass, an accessibility check, a fact-check workflow, five more Orders of Battle
- **A new default look, Sepia**: cream type on a dark page, a sepia map with the front drawn as a ticked black line, a serif face, and ruled lines in place of boxes. There is one red, kept for what needs a decision (the odds of a contested order, a seal, the stamp on an ending), and no portraits. Paper, Night and High contrast remain in the settings.

### Also in 1.3.0: 2026-10
### Added: a game that moves (map, readings, odds dial, sealed cables, war record, sound)
- **The front map is on every report**, small and drawn for that date, and can be expanded to the whole theatre. The countries are coloured by side (Central Powers, Entente, neutral) by date, the ground that changed hands is overlaid (Belgium and the north of France as the Western Front moves, Poland, the Baltic and Ukraine as the Eastern Front moves, Serbia, Romania, Friuli after Caporetto, Mesopotamia, Palestine, the Hejaz, the Caucasus), the front is a dashed line that marches, and the headquarters pulses. The Ottoman command has its own Near East map. The country outlines are Natural Earth's, already in the repository; the lines are drawn from the course of the fighting and are approximate, and the map says so.
- **Meanwhile.** Each other command's latest report up to the same date (the Russian dates brought to the Western calendar) is a pin on the map and a line in the expanded view.
- **Three readings under each meter**, after 1940: press Manpower, Munitions or the will meter to see what it is made of (Organisation, Experience and Readiness; Guns and Shells, Rail and Transport, Industry and Imports; the Army, the Government and the Public), each with a word for its state. They are tallied from where each order's impact fell, read from the order's own words, and average back to the headline. They change no roll and no ending.
- **Outcomes land.** After an order the gauges slide from the old figure to the new, and a list says what it did to each meter and to which reading ("Manpower −2: Readiness (Ready to Worn)"). A contested order's result comes back in a sealed cable that you break open.
- **Contested orders have an odds dial** (Long odds, Against us, Even, Favourable, Strong; the figure itself in easy mode) and are signed by holding the button for under a second. A keyboard or assistive press signs at once; "Tap to sign" in the settings turns the holding off.
- **The war record is rebuilt**: a service record (files closed, endings, decisions reached, the whole record as a bar, and for each command its progress, files closed and best rank), dossiers with portraits (a monogram for now), endings as stamped cards, an atlas with a year ribbon, and a battle record (how often each Order of Battle was fought and the best grade). The Echoes tab is gone; the marks themselves still work.

### Added: the Order of Battle (four battles), with a switch to take control of battle planning
- Four contested orders now open on a planning screen, ported in spirit from 1940 and 1941: the French attack on the Marne, the Fifth Army's dispositions at Gallipoli, the Fourth Army's plan for the Somme, and the South-Western Front's offensive in the summer of 1916. On the screen you place a pool of effort across four arms (each with its real formations), may name a commander who really led one, and choose an approach. The enemy's setup is drawn hidden; one line of intelligence points at it and is right about three times in four.
- The plan moves the odds of the order's roll toward the better outcome, up to 20 points either way and never out of the range 5 to 95. It is measured against the plan the staff would send without reading the intelligence: "Let the staff plan it" plays exactly the record's odds, a plan that reads the intelligence right beats it by up to about 15 points, and one that is wrong loses to it. The plan costs the meters a little (a heavy commitment costs that arm's meter a point; a win with no arm left short earns a point of will; never more than two points in all).
- The outcome carries an after-action report: the enemy's real setup, how far the plan moved the odds, how well the battle was fought (clean, costly, marginal or total) and what happened on the day in each arm. The next report adds a line about how the battle was fought. The pool is 6 chits, one more or less as the men and the shells stand.
- **Take control of battle planning** is a checkbox on the menu, on by default. Switched off, the four orders are rolled like any other, at the same odds as the staff's plan, with no screen and no report. Easy mode shows what each chit carries.
- 33 claims (the formations, dates and figures, from the pages read this session; the weights are game judgments and are said to be) and a validator, `check-battles.js`, that holds each battle to its rules: a real host with two outcomes, well-formed arms and setups, a staff plan that scores zero, informed plans that win something and wrong ones that lose something, and the echo. `tests/battle.test.mjs` plays the screens. ### Added: easy mode, command rank, glossary and strain
- **Easy mode** (a third switch on the menu, beside Standard and Hard mode). Each order shows what it will do to the three meters (a range, for a contested order), the order the command really gave is marked, and the last order can be taken back, up to forty times, also after a save and resume.
