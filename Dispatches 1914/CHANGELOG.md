# Dispatches 1914 changelog

Player-facing and rules-facing changes only (what a player or a balance check would notice). Newest first.
Add an entry in the same commit as the change. Engine changes live in packages/engine/CHANGELOG.md.
Format: version or date, then Fixed / Changed / Added. A change that moves the UI baseline must also be
listed in tests/baseline/known-diffs.json (see docs/WORKFLOW.md).

## Unreleased (2026-10, flow)
### Changed: the front of the game, one calendar, and a second wave of depth
- **The menu says which command is which.** The six commands are listed by side (the Central Powers, the Entente), each card led by its nation (Germany, France, Russia, Britain, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire), then the command's English name, its native name and seat, a line on what it asks of you, its dates and the number of decisions. How to play, the War record and Settings are three buttons under the commands.
- **A briefing before the war room.** Choosing a command opens a briefing: where things stand, your seat, what you will be asked, the map on the first day, what the three meters mean for that command, and then the set-up of the file: Easy, Standard or Hard mode, and whether to take control of battle planning. Both moved off the menu. A button enters the war room.
- **How to play** replaces "What this game leaves out" and Feedback on the menu: what you are doing, reading a report, orders and risk, battles, modes, endings, dates, and what the game leaves out as its last section. The feedback option and the author's note on the ending screen are gone.
- **Settings is a screen.** Text size is a slider with three stops (Standard, Larger, Largest) and a live sample; colours are swatches; type, signing and sound are grouped; the calendar is stated; one button returns every setting to its default.
- **One calendar.** Every date is written as in the UK (4 August 1914) and reckoned in the Western calendar. The Russian command's reports are converted from Old Style on screen, and its dates in the war record and the map. `check-briefings.js` fails a date written the American way or with an ordinal.
- **A report reads in order**: the decision, the wire, the situation, the map, the background, the meters, the orders.
- **Special events.** Thirteen events (the Turnip Winter, the April strikes of 1917, the casualty lists of 22 August, a new government in Paris, the shell shortage, the roads behind the front, Zeppelins over London, the Rising in Dublin, the death of the Emperor, the January strikes, the gaps in the railway, typhus in the army and others) come between two reports when the state of the file calls for them and the date is right. They are not decisions and change no meter; each is shown once.
- **A prelude for every command**, dated in the days before its first report: the Kaiser's question (1 August), ten kilometres on the French frontier (30 July), two offensives (Russia, 1 August), four divisions or six (5 August), mobilising against Russia (30 July) and the offer from Berlin (29 July). Each has three orders, and one is a roll.
- **Deeper forks.** Three-way decisions rise from 32 to 52 and contested orders from 34 to 46. Eleven more decisions have a third order (the Aisne, the Meuse reserves, Masuria, Przemysl, Naroch, Helles, conscription, the Oise, the Kolubara, the Hungarian divisions, Koprukoy); three new Ottoman reports (Romani, Baghdad, Gaza) take that command from 14 decisions to 18.
- **Four more Orders of Battle**: Tannenberg (Russian), the Chemin des Dames (French), Flers and Courcelette (British) and Ypres in November (German), 15 in all. 58 more claims, 12 of them for events.

## Unreleased (2026-10, forks)
### Added: many more forks, two more battles, a clearer report and outcome, a shareable file
- **More three-way decisions and more rolls.** Decisions with three orders rise from 3 to 32 and contested orders from 17 to 34. Each command gained a third, speculative order at several decisions, six of them leading into a short detour of their own (the German timetable in the east, the French week of the frontier battles, the Russian march on Lemberg, the British plateau at Passchendaele, the Austro-Hungarian flank in Galicia, the Ottoman clothing for the winter at Sarikamis) that rejoins the record. A detour begins "Speculative." and an order in it that returns to the record is the historical one. Several orders that were certain are now contested (the Aisne and the Douaumont counter-attack, Kornilov, the Sarikamis launch, the Suez attack, the Caporetto German request). New flags have readers further on, the endings' recaps list the new decisions, and 54 more claims are in the fact-check.
- **Caporetto and Megiddo as Orders of Battle** (Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman), on the German request for help and the order to hold in Palestine. Eleven battles now. Orders to hold or to attack that were fixed are now 75 to 25 and 85 to 15 rolls.
- **A few gates were retuned** so that an order other than the record's is not shut out by a meter alone (Verdun, Malmaison, the Marne, Lodz, Chantilly, Romania, Gallipoli, Erzurum). The record's own order is never gated.
- **Each report states its decision.** A line under the title says what is to be decided, with a link that jumps to the orders; the first letter of the report is the drop cap, not the title block. All 131 decisions have a line (`check-asks.js`).
- **An outcome says its verdict first**: whether it is as it happened, a modelled alternative or not as it happened, the headline, what the meters did as chips, the battle grade, then the text. A saved file resumes on the same outcome.
- **The Order of Battle is three steps** (officer, approach, counters) with a step list and a bar that stays in view: the counters left, the odds in words on the intelligence-shown posture, the staff's plan, and the button for the next step. The counters were called chits.
- **Share this file.** A closed file shows a card (command, ending and its badge, rank, orders given, contested, battles planned, departures from the record and the first three) that can be copied as text, saved as a picture (drawn in the browser, never uploaded) or printed.
- **Tools.** `npm run playthrough` plays every command to an ending in a real browser, three ways each (the record in easy mode, two seeded random ones with a battle planned by hand and by the staff, in different themes), with an accessibility check on the way; it runs in CI. `node tools/balance.mjs` plays 4,000 files by six styles of player and writes docs/BALANCE.md; the findings are in docs/BALANCE_NOTES.md.

- **A new default look, Sepia**: cream type on a dark page, a sepia map with the front drawn as a ticked black line, a serif face, and ruled lines in place of boxes. There is one red, kept for what needs a decision (the odds of a contested order, a seal, the stamp on an ending), and no portraits. Paper, Night and High contrast remain in the settings.

A Type setting offers the look's own face, the typewriter, or a plain, clear one. Every colour pair in every command is checked for contrast by `check-themes.js` (4.5:1 for text, 7:1 in High contrast). A second **Type** setting offers a plain, clear face in place of the typewriter. Both are in the settings and are kept with the other settings.
- **A phone pass.** Tap targets are at least 44 pixels, the expanded map opens as a sheet from the bottom, text wraps and the page never scrolls sideways, and the notch and home bar are respected. Hold-to-sign tolerates a thumb that drifts a little and buzzes when the order is signed.
- **Accessibility.** The expanded map keeps the keyboard focus inside it, closes on Escape and gives the focus back; the focus goes on to the cable once the seal is broken. `npm run audit:screens` plays the menu, a report in each command, the readings, the map, a battle, the sealed cable, the outcome and the war record in each theme on a phone and checks each for axe violations, tap size, sideways scrolling and console errors (Chromium, and WebKit with `BROWSERS=chromium,webkit`).

- **Five more Orders of Battle**: Galicia 1914 (Austro-Hungarian), the March offensive of 1918 (German), Verdun (German), Kut (Ottoman) and the Piave in June 1918 (Austro-Hungarian), each on an order that was already contested, so no existing outcome changes. 29 more claims. (Caporetto and Megiddo follow below.)

## Unreleased (2026-10)
### Added: a game that moves (map, readings, odds dial, sealed cables, war record, sound)
- **The front map is on every report**, small and drawn for that date, and can be expanded to the whole theatre. The countries are coloured by side (Central Powers, Entente, neutral) by date, the ground that changed hands is overlaid (Belgium and the north of France as the Western Front moves, Poland, the Baltic and Ukraine as the Eastern Front moves, Serbia, Romania, Friuli after Caporetto, Mesopotamia, Palestine, the Hejaz, the Caucasus), the front is a dashed line that marches, and the headquarters pulses. The Ottoman command has its own Near East map. The country outlines are Natural Earth's, already in the repository; the lines are drawn from the course of the fighting and are approximate, and the map says so.
- **Meanwhile.** Each other command's latest report up to the same date (the Russian dates brought to the Western calendar) is a pin on the map and a line in the expanded view.
- **Three readings under each meter**, after 1940: press Manpower, Munitions or the will meter to see what it is made of (Organisation, Experience and Readiness; Guns and Shells, Rail and Transport, Industry and Imports; the Army, the Government and the Public), each with a word for its state. They are tallied from where each order's impact fell, read from the order's own words, and average back to the headline. They change no roll and no ending.
- **Outcomes land.** After an order the gauges slide from the old figure to the new, and a list says what it did to each meter and to which reading ("Manpower −2: Readiness (Ready to Worn)"). A contested order's result comes back in a sealed cable that you break open.
- **Contested orders have an odds dial** (Long odds, Against us, Even, Favourable, Strong; the figure itself in easy mode) and are signed by holding the button for under a second. A keyboard or assistive press signs at once; "Tap to sign" in the settings turns the holding off.
- **The war record is rebuilt**: a service record (files closed, endings, decisions reached, the whole record as a bar, and for each command its progress, files closed and best rank), dossiers with portraits (a monogram for now), endings as stamped cards, an atlas with a year ribbon, and a battle record (how often each Order of Battle was fought and the best grade). The Echoes tab is gone; the marks themselves still work.
- **Sound** has a third setting, Atmosphere: an ambience for the command in play (distant guns, wind, rain, made in the browser, no files) and small sounds for a bulletin on the wire, a seal, a signature and an order held down. It is off by default and silent where the browser has no audio.
- Prototypes only, not in the game yet: an engraved scene (the railhead) and an adviser card, in `art/`.

### Changed: a new look, one paper and one hand for each command
- Each command's screens take its own paper tint, accent and a coloured band at the head of the sheet (black, white and red for the German command; the tricolour for the French; black, gold and white for the Russian; khaki dashes for the British; black and yellow for the Austro-Hungarian; violet beads for the Ottoman), with a double-ruled letterhead and the seal.
- The meters are a ledger: the figure, and a gauge from loss to gain with a marker. The date is a rubber date stamp, the first letter of each report is a drop cap, orders are numbered slips with a coloured edge, the bulletin is a wire slip pasted to the sheet, the folders on the menu have tabs, the map is framed, and an ending is stamped (the stamp comes down only when the system allows motion).
- The typewriter and the serif have fuller font stacks (American Typewriter, Courier Prime, Courier; Iowan Old Style, Palatino). The page is a desk with the sheet laid on it. Text sizes, contrast and the phone layout are kept; the release check (axe, four browsers) passes. No text or behaviour changed: all 144 baseline runs are identical.

### Added: the Order of Battle (four battles), with a switch to take control of battle planning
- Four contested orders now open on a planning screen, ported in spirit from 1940 and 1941: the French attack on the Marne, the Fifth Army's dispositions at Gallipoli, the Fourth Army's plan for the Somme, and the South-Western Front's offensive in the summer of 1916. On the screen you place a pool of effort across four arms (each with its real formations), may name a commander who really led one, and choose an approach. The enemy's setup is drawn hidden; one line of intelligence points at it and is right about three times in four.
- The plan moves the odds of the order's roll toward the better outcome, up to 20 points either way and never out of the range 5 to 95. It is measured against the plan the staff would send without reading the intelligence: "Let the staff plan it" plays exactly the record's odds, a plan that reads the intelligence right beats it by up to about 15 points, and one that is wrong loses to it. The plan costs the meters a little (a heavy commitment costs that arm's meter a point; a win with no arm left short earns a point of will; never more than two points in all).
- The outcome carries an after-action report: the enemy's real setup, how far the plan moved the odds, how well the battle was fought (clean, costly, marginal or total) and what happened on the day in each arm. The next report adds a line about how the battle was fought. The pool is 6 chits, one more or less as the men and the shells stand.
- **Take control of battle planning** is a checkbox on the menu, on by default. Switched off, the four orders are rolled like any other, at the same odds as the staff's plan, with no screen and no report. Easy mode shows what each chit carries.
- 33 claims (the formations, dates and figures, from the pages read this session; the weights are game judgments and are said to be) and a validator, `check-battles.js`, that holds each battle to its rules: a real host with two outcomes, well-formed arms and setups, a staff plan that scores zero, informed plans that win something and wrong ones that lose something, and the echo. `tests/battle.test.mjs` plays the screens. Two more battles (Galicia in 1914 and the German offensive of March 1918) are the obvious next ones.

### Added: the Ottoman General Staff (13 decisions, 5 endings)
- The sixth command is now playable: the Ottoman war ministry and general staff, from the German ships at the Dardanelles (August 1914) to the armistice of Mudros (October 1918). Thirteen decisions: the Goeben and the Breslau, the Black Sea raid, the winter offensive at Sarikamis, the attack on the Suez Canal, where the landing comes at Gallipoli (a contested roll), Kut (a contested roll), Erzurum, the railway to Medina, Baghdad or Palestine, Jerusalem, the road to Baku, the line before Megiddo and the terms at Lemnos. Five endings: the armistice (the record), the Straits lost, a smaller war, the last line in the interior, and (hard mode) the dismissal of the minister.
- The meter is Imperial Control. Hard mode tracks commitments beyond what the railways and the stores could carry (Sarikamis, Medina and Baku on the record, and the plans to retake Baghdad and to defend Jerusalem): about one run in six ends in the minister's dismissal. Measured over 20,000 random runs: the armistice 58%, the last line 23%, a smaller war 12%, the Straits lost 7%.
- A second map, of Anatolia, the Levant, Mesopotamia and the Caucasus, drawn from the same public-domain Natural Earth outline; the front map shows each command's own.
- **The Armenian genocide** is narrated as settled fact in the node for 24 April 1915, on the night it began, and again in the closing epilogues: the government, its ruling party and units of the army carried it out, and the command whose file it is bears its share of the responsibility. It is never a choice, a flag or a meter effect, and nothing in the file can alter it (the smoke test checks that no Ottoman choice, label, flag or bulletin is about it). "What this game leaves out" says the same.
- Dates are Western; the Rumi calendar (Julian, with the year from 1 March) is explained, with one Rumi date, in the second node. What the General Staff itself dated its papers in is not confirmed and is recorded as open.
- 109 claims in `claims/otto.json` (76 `web-checked`, the rest mostly the advisers' positions, marked `drafted`), 20 new sources, 6 new glossary entries (52 in all), 24 new baseline recordings, 2 new save fixtures. The validators now pass with the Ottoman research gate closed.

### Added: six more echoes between commands

The historical lines still reach Brest-Litovsk and the armistice of Villa Giusti in both modes.

### Added: easy mode, command rank, glossary and strain
- **Easy mode** (a third switch on the menu, beside Standard and Hard mode). Each order shows what it will do to the three meters (a range, for a contested order), the order the command really gave is marked, and the last order can be taken back, up to forty times, also after a save and resume. It is named for a famous machine of each army on the command's card: Big Bertha (German OHL), Soixante-Quinze (French GQG), Ilya Muromets (Russian Stavka), Mother (British Empire) and Skoda (Austro-Hungarian AOK).
- **A command rank on every ending**, a score out of 100 with its five parts shown: standing at the close (the three meters together, 30), the ending (a tier of 0 to 3 given to every ending, 30), nothing run dry (15), restraint (orders that cost the command standing, 10) and the mode (easy 5, standard 10, hard 15), from Staff Captain to Field Marshal. An easy file cannot rise above General. The tiers are the game's own judgment of how each ending left the army and the state, not a historical claim; `check-rank.js` keeps every ending covered.
- **A glossary**: 46 words and places (corps, salient, blockade, Galicia, Isonzo, Doullens, the Hindenburg Programme and so on). On each screen the first mention of a term in the story text is underlined, and pressing it shows the definition; the War Record has a Glossary tab. People are not in it: they have dossiers. `check-glossary.js` checks every term is used and none is defined twice.
- **"What this game leaves out"** on the menu: the war outside the commands, the people the orders fell on, and the crimes that are not decisions a general could take, among them the killing of Armenians in the Ottoman Empire from 1915 (named with the source: the International Association of Genocide Scholars, 1997).
- **Strain on contested orders.** Below -2, each point a meter is short moves 3 points of the odds from the best outcome of a contested order to its worst (at most 15, and never below 5 on the best), counted on the meter the order is about, and the order says so ("Strain: Manpower is short, so the odds are 6 points worse"). It touches only the contested rolls; settled outcomes are still narrated, never rolled. Monte Carlo (20,000 runs a command): every ending is still reached, with no dead end or softlock.
- Saves now also carry the easy flag, the orders given and the take-back history. All three are optional, so a save from 1.2.0 still resumes (as a standard or hard run with nothing to take back); `SAVE_SCHEMA_VERSION` is unchanged.
- `tests/easy-mode.test.mjs` (the preview, the mark, the take-back, save and resume, the rank panel, a glossary term) and 40 easy-mode runs in the UI baseline (one for each command and seed). The UI baseline was re-recorded: the only differences from before are the rank panel on every ending screen and the strain notice and odds on contested orders (checked by switching strain off: then only the ending screens differ).

### Added: look, sound, access and feedback
- Ending badges are stamped, and look different for the three kinds of ending (a plain frame for settled, a double frame for contested, a dashed frame for speculative), so the difference does not rely on colour.
- A Sound setting (off by default): a typewriter tick on each order and a stamp when a file closes, made with the browser's own audio; nothing is downloaded.
- Each new screen puts focus on its heading, so keyboard and screen-reader users hear the change.
- A Feedback section on the menu and "A note for the author" on every ending: a line recording the path taken, ready to paste into a comment on the game's page. docs/PLAYTEST.md says how to run a playtest and, plainly, that none has been run.
- `art/cover.html` and `art/cover.png`: the cover for the itch.io page (630 by 500), in the game's own palette.
### Added: the front map
- Every decision has a map under "Show the map": the stretch of Europe where the file has been, with the route of the headquarters so far and the current place ringed. Only places already visited are shown. The outline is Natural Earth land data (public domain); every node's city has a coordinate (`check-maps.js`).
- A stray double arrow on the "Show background" control is gone.
### Added: echoes between commands
- Six choices leave a mark that another command reads: submarine warfare (German) reaches the British convoy debate, the 1918 manpower question and the French army in 1917; Allied command in 1918 (French or British) reaches the German staff in May and August; the French counterattack on the Marne reaches the German staff in September 1914; the British under Nivelle reaches the French staff; the German plan for 1915 reaches the Austro-Hungarian staff; German help for Vienna reaches the Austro-Hungarian staff in 1917.
- Each echo is written only for the departure from the record, so a player who follows history never sees one (a smoke test checks every node under every historical echo value). Marks are kept in the war record and become the starting flags of the next run; the War Record has a new Echoes tab.
### Added: attested quotations
- Nine choices carry a short line of attested wording, shown as "On the record" with its source (Haig's order of 11 April 1918, Ludendorff on the black day, Foch at Doullens, Clemenceau, Kitchener's telegram, Dukhonin's reply, Karl's letter to Sixtus, Wilson's telegram and Haig's reply). Advisers still speak in indirect speech. `check-quotations.js` requires a source and a logged claim for each; the quotations are in the fact-check worksheet.
### Content: the writing pass
- Every choice outcome on the three commands is now 60 to 85 words (110 were under 60): the consequence is followed through to what the command has to live with next, in the same register. Counterfactual outcomes still begin "Speculative." and add only consequences that follow from the change.
- Every ending's epilogue now ends with a paragraph headed "What actually happened" (the real sequel: dates, signatories, what the armistice or treaty required), 100 to 200 words in all. The 27 paragraphs are logged as claims, marked drafted, for the fact-check.
- `check-prose-length.js` (in `validate.sh`) keeps outcomes at 55 to 100 words and epilogues at 100 or more.
