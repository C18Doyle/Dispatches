# Dispatches 1922 changelog

Player-facing and rules-facing changes only (what a player or a balance check would notice). Newest first.
Add an entry in the same commit as the change. Engine changes live in packages/engine/CHANGELOG.md.
Format: version or date, then Fixed / Changed / Added. A change that moves the UI baseline must also be
listed in tests/baseline/known-diffs.json (see docs/WORKFLOW.md).

## Unreleased (2026-10)
### Changed
- Advisers no longer speak. All 160 invented quotations in South Russia, Siberia and the Bolsheviks are rewritten as third-person positions ("Wrangel argues that..."), as the 1917 campaign already was, following docs/WRITING.md rule 7. Invented figures and first-hand claims in the old lines were dropped. tools/check-advisers.mjs fails on any adviser line that is a quote, has no position, starts with a speech mark, uses an em dash or speaks in the first person, and is part of test:fast.
- One calendar. Every date is Western and written day then month (7 November 1917, not October 25 and not 25 October Old Style). The 1917 campaign is converted: the Miliukov note is now May, the Kerensky Offensive July, the Kornilov affair September, and the rising falls on 6 and 7 November, so those reports carry the new months in the timeline and on the map stepper. The 1917 reports lose their Old Style dates; the How to Read screen says so once. US-order dates in the other campaigns (July 29) are reordered.
- Hard mode: South Russia and the Bolsheviks can now reach their capital cap, so The Mutiny and The Central Committee Moves can fire. Wrangel's dismissal and the Novorossiysk evacuation order (South Russia) and the Tsaritsyn reassertion, the intensified requisitioning and the unified Cavalry Army (Bolsheviks) now cost capital. Measured with tools/capital-reach.mjs on greedy play: South Russia reaches 5 on about 25% of runs (3 or 4 before), the Bolsheviks on about 66% (3 before).
- Layout is mobile-first: the whole app is a centred column at most 600px wide on desktop (it was full width). Phones are unchanged.
- tools/monte-carlo.js replaced by tools/monte-carlo.mjs, which plays the real src/logic.ts (seeded). It reports gate-bite rate and which endings are reached. It no longer reproduces the old routing.
### Added
- A spine check (tools/historical-spine.mjs, `npm run check:spine`): follows the historical choice at every report on the real rules and fails if the record cannot be followed to a historical ending; also reports how often random play stays on the record.
- A claims register for the whole game: 468 checkable statements (dates, figures, units, named events, adviser biographies) logged in claims/<campaign>.json with a status and sources, claims/sources.json (63 sources, each marked page-read or search-summary), claims/uncovered.json, a reviewer page claims/review.html and tools to apply a reviewer's answers (tools/claims-draft.mjs, check-claims.mjs, claims-review.mjs, apply-review.mjs; claims/README.md). In the first pass 84 were web-checked and 19 marked disputed; the rest are drafted and unchecked. check:claims is part of test:fast and fails on a stale claim, an unknown source or a report with no claims.
- Four more Orders of Battle, so every command has at least one: Chelyabinsk (July 1919) and Irkutsk (1920) for Siberia, Voronezh and Kastornoye (1919) and Perekop (1920) for the Bolsheviks. Two hosting orders were given a roll: the counterattack at Chelyabinsk (the documented defeat stays the likelier outcome, 75%) and the Sivash crossing at Perekop (the documented success stays the likelier, 70%; the other outcome leads to The Ports, and Kronstadt reads which it was). An echo can now name several reports. Sources, and one open question about the Chelyabinsk text (Diterichs), in claims/battles-round2.md.
- Orders of Battle. An order that is a real engagement opens a planning screen: name an officer, choose an approach, place a pool of counters across the arms (the pool is 4 to 8, by the state of the meters). The enemy's setup is drawn hidden and one line of intelligence, right three times in four, points at it. The plan moves the odds of the order's roll toward the better outcome (at most 20 points, weights kept inside 5 to 95), charges the meters up to two points in all, and the next report echoes how it was fought. "Let the staff plan it" plays the record's own odds exactly; so does switching "Take control of battle planning" off on the war-room screen. Four battles: the June Offensive (1917), Ekaterinodar (1918), Peregonovka and Orel (1919). Easy mode shows what each counter is worth. src/battle.ts (typed, ported from 1914), src/parts/35-battles.jsx, tests/battle.test.mjs, sources in claims/battles-round1.md.
- The front map has a month stepper ("Earlier month" and "Later month"): the control of each place is shown as of any month this run has been through. Later months stay closed.
- Key Events has a second view, "The war at large": what every command was deciding, month by month, up to the month the run has reached, built from the four situation atlases (a new report appears in it with no extra work). Later months and the player's own unvisited branches stay hidden. tests/timeline.test.mjs.
- Easy mode, one of three ways to open a command (with Standard and the hard mode). Each command has a name of its own: Tachanka Command (South Russia), Teplushka Command (Siberia), Armoured Train Command (Bolsheviks), Samovar Command (1917). Every order shows its effect on the meters, a contested order lists every outcome with its odds, and the last order can be taken back (the report returns exactly as it was, and an ending seen only in the outcome is not left discovered). The saved run records the mode. tests/easy-mode.test.mjs; 24 Easy runs in the UI baseline, two of them taking an order back.
- Accessibility pass: a main landmark, a level-1 heading on every screen, the meters exposed as meters (role meter with value and text), the campaign cards and the objectives and background panels reachable and usable from the keyboard, a named close control, focus moved to the page and the view scrolled to the top on each new screen, reduced motion taken from the system setting (and the typewriter off with it), and two accent colours made readable (the Agitprop red and the Telegram gold were under 4.5:1). tools/check-a11y.mjs plays four runs and the menus and fails on any of these; it is part of test:fast.
- The 1917 Provisional Government campaign grows from 4 reports to 10: the May commissars and Soldiers' Declaration, the Supreme Command (Kornilov's terms), the Moscow State Conference, the September Directory, the Democratic Conference and the morning of 24 October. Three reports have three choices. Adviser lines in this campaign are positions, not speech: the eight old quotes were rewritten the same way. New adviser dossier: Savinkov. Sources in claims/provisional-government-round1.md; several lines are marked there as not yet read.
- The 1917 hard mode (Decree Capital) can now be played to its end: six choices cost capital, and the fifth point ends the campaign at a new ending, The Cabinet That Governed Alone (speculative). Before, no 1917 choice cost capital, and the hard-mode ending was The General's Republic.
- South Russia: a new report, "Sevastopol: The Radio From Frunze" (11 November 1920), with three choices (keep the offer from the army, refuse it, publish it). The ending epilogue records what happened. Sources in claims/south-russia-round1.md.
- South Russia: a third choice at the Sevastopol council (name the successor by order after hearing the council).
- Adviser lines can be written as a third-person position (no speech marks) with `advisor.position`; new text uses it (docs/WRITING.md rule 7).
- tools/capital-reach.mjs: how much hard-mode capital a run can spend, and which reports offer it.
- Save migrations and node aliases (`NODE_ALIASES`, `SAVE_MIGRATIONS`, `migrateSave`) so an update upgrades saves instead of wiping them (docs/SAVES.md).
- `check:orphans`: every listed node and ending is reachable (hard-mode endings excepted).
- Save-compatibility test (npm run test:saves) with four committed old saves.

### Fixed (fact-check pass, see claims/*.json)
- Kiev, 31 August 1919: the Ukrainian parade was planned for the afternoon, not the morning.
- Orel: about 200 miles from Moscow (the text said 250 and 200), the high-water mark of the advance (not "the deepest"), retaken after seven days (not eight).
- Peregonovka: a retreat of some 600 km (not "a four-hundred-mile pursuit"). Kolchak's offensive came within reach of the Volga; it did not reach it.
- Czechoslovak Legion in 1918: some 40,000 (not 50,000); the Chelyabinsk clash is five weeks after the Ekaterinodar assault (not six). In 1919 "tens of thousands", not 60,000.
- Kornilov was killed at his farmhouse headquarters outside Ekaterinodar (not at Elizavetinskaya).
- Novorossiysk: only five or six thousand people could be taken off by the British ships (not "at a time"); the claim that General Holman supervised the embarkation on the mole is removed (no source); the Red cavalry is not called Voroshilov's. Holman is a major-general and left South Russia with Denikin in April 1920.
- Wrangel was dismissed by an order of 8 February 1920 and reached Constantinople at the end of the month; the claim that Lukomsky and Shatilov were dismissed with him is removed. Shatilov's death date (unverified) is removed.
- Ulagai's landing: several thousand men, 14 August to 7 September (the text said 4,500 and "barely three weeks").
- Denikin was named Kolchak's successor on 4 January 1920 and accepted neither the title nor its functions (the text said December 1919 and that he held the title).
- Krivoshein joined Wrangel in the spring of 1920 and headed his government from June.
- Omsk, December 1918: the rising was a Bolshevik underground rising on 22 December, and the documented killing is nine Socialist-Revolutionary members of the Constituent Assembly shot from the prison (the text said a Left SR rising and 500 executed).
- Chelyabinsk 1919: the Red army was the 5th under Tukhachevsky (the text said Frunze's 3rd); Kappel died on 26 January 1920 (one source 25 January; the text said both); Voitsekhovsky was evacuated with Wrangel's army from the Crimea (not via Vladivostok); Sakharov was replaced in December 1919 and his unverified death date is removed.
- The 1st Cavalry Army was formed in the second half of November 1919; Voroshilov and Shchadenko joined its council in December. Lwow is about 200 miles from Warsaw (not 250). The Red Army lost the battle of Warsaw (the text said "Warsaw was lost").
- 1917: three Old Style dates the calendar change had missed (the Directory on 14 September, the Soviet's resolution on 13 September, the Central Committee vote on 23 October).
### Fixed
- The Bolsheviks ending "A Hollow Victory" could never be reached: it required `requisitionPolicy: intensified` and `congressChoice: press`, which are set on opposite branches of the campaign. It now follows an assault on Kronstadt after either intensified requisitioning or pressing the Eighth Congress (about 9% of random Bolsheviks runs). Five seeded baseline runs now end there instead of at The Ice Broken (recorded in tests/baseline/known-diffs.json).
- A 9px muted header label ("FILE NO. 1922") was below the 4.5:1 contrast ratio; its colour is slightly darker.
