# South Russia, round 1: sources for new text

Facts written into `frunzeOffer20` (11 November 1920) and the third choice at `sevastopolCouncil20` (April 1920).
Each line says what the game asserts, where it was read, and what is still open.

| Claim in the game | Source read | Status |
|---|---|---|
| On 11 Nov 1920 Frunze radioed Wrangel an offer to stop fighting, with amnesty for those who laid down arms | Lenin, Collected Works, telegram of 12 Nov 1920 and editorial note (marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1920/nov/12.htm) | read |
| Wrangel did not reply and did not tell his troops | Same editorial note | read; Soviet-era editors |
| The offer was rejected | Presidential Library of Russia, anniversary piece on Frunze (prlib.ru/en/news/2022906) | read; conflicts with the line above, so the game says "the sources disagree" |
| Lenin thought the terms excessively accommodating and wanted the fleet seized if they were accepted | Lenin telegram, same page | read |
| Last personnel left the Crimea on 14 Nov 1920 | US Navy documentary history, 16 Nov 1920 report (history.navy.mil, post-war humanitarian series) | read |
| The generals at Sevastopol thought it subversive of discipline for subordinates to elect their superior and named Wrangel as the only acceptable successor; Denikin then designated him | US naval telegrams from Admiral McCully, April 1920 (as summarised in search results) | secondary; the date (3 or 4 April) differs between sources, so the game gives no day |
| General Dragomirov's committee asked Wrangel to return | Single source | not asserted as fact; the game says accounts differ |

Rejected after checking, so no one proposes them again:

- A Kuban or Donbass third option at `northernTauride20`: the Wikipedia article on the Northern Taurida Operation describes no weighed alternatives in June 1920, and no source found shows a staff debate.
- A Tuapse/Sochi/Georgia route as a third option at `novorossiysk20`: the Novorossiysk sources read say nothing on it.
- Alekseev or Romanovsky deciding the retreat from Ekaterinodar: the sources credit Denikin alone.
