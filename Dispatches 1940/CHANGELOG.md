# Dispatches 1940 changelog

Player-facing and rules-facing changes only (what a player or a balance check would notice). Newest first.
Add an entry in the same commit as the change. Engine changes live in packages/engine/CHANGELOG.md.
Format: version or date, then Fixed / Changed / Added. A change that moves the UI baseline must also be
listed in tests/baseline/known-diffs.json (see docs/WORKFLOW.md).

## 6.1.0 (2026-10-10)
### Changed
- **"Take Control of Battle Planning" is a stamp on the war room page**, next to Historically Accurate Opponent and drawn the same way, ticked by default. Lift it and the battles are not played: a choice that would open the Order of Battle is made like any other decision (no "Leads to battle planning" badge, no planning screen or report, an outcome straight away), and strain bears on it like any contested choice. The setting is kept in the save (an older save without it plans its battles, as before). `tests/battle-toggle.test.mjs`, in `test:fast`, plays the same seeded run both ways, then saves, resumes and checks an older save.
- **The meter panels open closed on every new game.** Which panel was open used to be stored for good, so one opened once stayed open at the start of every later game. They now stay as you leave them within a game and start closed with the next one.
- **The bars are plainer.** The word "Low" under a meter that has fallen to -2 to -4 is gone ("Dangerous" and "Critical" stay), and so are the black lines on the bars: the centre line and the tick that marked where the bar was before the last decision. The bar still slides from the old reading, and a screen reader is still told which way it moved.
- **The meters are on the outcome page and the battle planning page again.** After a decision the outcome page shows where you stand now, with the three bars sliding from their old reading and the three readings under each; the Order of Battle planning page opens with the same panel. They are shown in every mode except the Führer's, which has no dashboard by design.
- **The easy modes have their names back** (Elefant, T-35, Defiant and Breda Command), and the war room page shows them as "Easy: Breda Command", "Normal: Standard Issue Command" and "Hard: Führer Mode" and so on.
- **The Holocaust section is written more carefully, comes last in the title screen's list, and names Yad Vashem as its only source.**
- **The main menu and the war room, shorter.** The line under the title is one sentence ("Choose a high command and endeavor to lead your armies to victory."), each command card gives its first sentence and the number of decisions and nothing else, and the difficulty screen is gone: the war room now holds the choice. Normal is selected; Easy and Hard (named for the command: the Führer, the NKVD, Yalta, Axis) are one tap away, each with a one-line description. The paragraph of context and the unattributed German line that stood in that page are removed, and the real quotations stay.
- **The meters are green when they are up and red when they are down**, and just show the figure ("-1"); the "was 0" line is gone. The strategic review that sat between the map button and the meters is now inside the theater map.
- **The map at the end of the war is the theater map.** The end screen showed the older schematic board (the circles and dotted lines); it now shows the same map as the rest of the game, set at the day the war ended (Germany divided at the end of 1945, for instance), with the scrubber back through the run and every divergence from the record listed under it. The "tap the territory for why" hints are removed.
- **Battles cost less, and what is owed is no longer shown.** Random play fell to -8 or worse within five decisions on the German and Soviet commands (43% and 57% of the run). A battle's plan now costs a meter at most 1 and the three meters at most 2 in all; the same random play now spends 25% and 37% of the run that low. Debt below the floor is still remembered (the cap is 3 points now, and gains still pay it first) but is not shown anywhere.
- **Strain names the meter the decision is about.** A contested decision was strained by whichever meter was lowest, usually Manpower, whatever the decision was. It is now strained by the meter its check names, or else the one its outcomes move most, and says so.
- **Norway is German from 3 May 1940** (contested only while the Allies were fighting in central Norway), and on the path where the garrison outlasts the war it stays occupied, not contested. The Case Blue "meanwhile" line no longer tells the player what Stavka will get wrong, and two other "meanwhile" lines are plain.
- **No em dashes in the on-screen text** (about 2,900 replaced with commas, colons or brackets, by the sense of the sentence). The rules are in `docs/WRITING.md`; `npm run check-writing` fails on a new one.
- **Three readings under every meter, and the three bars never change size.** Matériel had four readings under it and the other two meters three; it now has three, like them: Fuel & Oil, Arms & Ammunition (Ammunition and Armour & Steel, joined) and Shipping & Rail. A battle arm that drew on ammunition or on steel now reads Arms & Ammunition; old saves carry their ammunition and steel tallies over to it. The figure column beside each bar is now one width whatever it holds, and what a decision just changed ("was +1") and any warning ("Dangerous") sit on a line of their own under the bar, which is kept even when empty, so the bar is the same length at every moment (it used to shorten when a "was" appeared). The line explaining positive and negative under the meters is gone.
- **The command rank is fair to a war that could not be won.** The ending is worth 30 of the 100 points and is now scored against the best ending open to that command's path (the table is `ENDING_CEILING` in `src/logic.ts`): a German command's best is a minor victory, the Italian co-belligerent path's a contested outcome, the Salò Republic's a minor defeat; Soviet and Allied commands are scored as before. Before, a flawless Italian co-belligerent command topped out at 78 (Colonel; General needs 83) and a flawless Salò command lower still. The rank panel now says what the best open ending was. `npm run check-endings` (in `audit` and `test:fast`) fails if any ending outranks its path's ceiling.
- **Italy's meters no longer sit on the floor, and its endings are no longer one ending.** Random play used to reach Salerno with Manpower at -9 and Initiative at -8, and 97% of co-belligerent endings (91% of Salò's) were "An Army Spent Twice". Twenty-seven impacts before the armistice were rebalanced: the choices that conserve forces (a symbolic advance, trading space for time, a withdrawal, standing an operation down, halting at the frontier, a negotiated surrender, a fighting withdrawal) recover a point of Manpower, no choice costs two points of Initiative, the worst Manpower costs are a point shallower, and a few historical choices no longer cost Initiative. Random play now reaches Salerno near -4 and -4; "An Army Spent Twice" is 44% of co-belligerent endings and 11% of Salò's; it now needs the meter's own Exhausted line (-8, it was -6), and "The Command That Lost the Least" (+3, it was +6 and never produced) can be reached. Following history still wears the army out completely, as it should.
- **`npm run check-endings`** prints how often each ending occurs, per campaign (Italy by path), for random play and for play that always takes the historical choice, and fails if one ending takes more than 85% of a group's random-play runs (it warns above 60%).
- **Meters have depth.** Next to Manpower, Matériel and Initiative on the decision page there is now an expand button. Open, each shows its micro-states with a status word: Manpower is Organisation, Experience and Readiness; Matériel keeps its four strands (Fuel & Oil, Ammunition, Armour & Steel, Shipping & Rail); Initiative is Intelligence, Command and Tempo. A micro-state that moved since the last decision carries a green or red arrow (only when its word changed or it moved by two or more). The staff's notes on a meter (reserves at breaking point, matériel exhausted) moved in beside it. The panel remembers which meters you had open. Each micro-state is fed by what the choice was about (the same text-reading rule the Matériel strands used), and `npm run check-materiel-strands` now audits all three meters.
- **A fifth, worse band.** A meter at -8 or below reads Exhausted (and every micro-state under it), with a "Critical" tag, in red, beside the meter, and a stronger staff note, instead of "Short" on everything.
- **Debts below the floor.** A meter stops at -10, and a long run of costs once it was there used to vanish: one good decision then read -9. What a decision takes below -10 is now owed (up to 6), shown as "N owed" on the meter, and gains pay the debt before they raise the meter.
- **Shortages change the odds.** A meter below -4 takes points of probability off the best outcome of every contested decision (not battles, which weigh their own shortages), and each point owed adds half a point more, up to 12. The decision says so in red before you choose ("Strain: Matériel short, so the odds are 7 points worse for it"), and the roll uses the same weights.
- **A battle no longer charges the same fault twice.** On a loss, an arm left uncovered also cost its meter a point, on top of the lower odds it had already cost and the grade it had already set. That charge is gone. And where the outcome itself already costs a meter two points or more, the plan's own charge on that meter is held to one. Measured over every battle and a spread of plans: an average lost battle cost 5.8 across the meters (2.8 from the outcome, 3.0 from the plan), against 1.8 for an ordinary decision, and the plan's charge landed on a meter the outcome had already hit in 65% of losses; it now costs 4.5. "Heavy commitment" (-1 on the arm's meter, win or lose) is unchanged and is now the biggest remaining charge.
- **Choose a command, then a difficulty.** The main screen lists the four commands; a click opens a second screen with the three difficulties side by side as cards of the same rows (meter impact shown first, the historical choice marked, rewind, dashboard, intelligence in battle, an extra tracker, orders from above in battle, reports censored as Manpower falls), a mark where a row differs from Standard. The War Room says plainly what the chosen command changes.
- **Hard-mode banners.** "No rewind" is no longer on every decision page (the War Room and the difficulty screen say it). Coalition Cohesion (Yalta Mode) and German Trust (Axis Mode) are counters on their own scale, -6 to +6 and -5 to +5, with the point at which the command is lost written beside them; Suspicion and political capital show their count out of five.
- **"Always let my staff plan battles"** is a setting on the main screen (off by default), not a checkbox in the battle.
- **The planning screen** is tidier: "How it works" and "Let Your Staff Plan It" sit side by side at the top; the staff's situation report and the order of battle are one disclosure under each arm; "what actually happened" is no longer shown while you plan (it is under the verdict, after the battle); the Map Exercise and the Staff Assessment are one "Ask the staff to review the plan" for one Initiative; the category symbols (the plane and the rest) are gone; every battle screen is in the same typeface.
- **The battle report runs on its own.** "Start battle" sits at the top. Dispatches then arrive by themselves, the newest at the top and the older ones moving down, with a pause between them and the front-line bar swinging as each lands. A field decision, the decisive hour or the counterattack stops the battle until you answer. There is a Pause, and "Skip to the verdict"; with instant text or reduced motion on there is no waiting between dispatches. Resuming a saved battle starts the report again from "Start battle".
- **The decisive hour and the counterattack say what they are asking.** The decisive hour says where the line stands, which arms are carrying the attack and which are short, what committed and held effort are each worth, and the counterattack's panel says where it will fall, how many points it takes to throw it back, and what each choice, including throwing in the reserve, would mean as things stand.
- **The end screen is one story, not five tables.** The ending and its aftermath, the **command rank** (Private, Corporal, Sergeant, Lieutenant, Captain, Major, Colonel, General, with the five things it was built from and a word on each), where the command ended, advisers, objectives, and one "How You Commanded" section whose decisions open on request. The dice paragraph, the timeline, the scorecard and the always-open decision log are folded into it. The rank is built from the ending's tier, the command's condition, the battles (a staff-planned battle counts a little less), judgement against the historical choices and the objectives; luck in the dice is not scored; a harder mode scores higher and the training-wheels mode cannot reach General; being removed from command holds the rank at Lieutenant. The War Record shows the rank beside each recent war, and the copyable After-Action Summary is plain lines in the order a reader wants them.
- **The paper no longer fakes damage.** The brown stains, the handwritten margin note ("check losses - unsustainable?") and the fading of the text with Matériel are gone: players took them for bugs or for advice, and the meters now say the same thing in words.
- **The strategic review** is one line under the theatre map ("Strategic review - behind the historical baseline - history matched at 5 of 12 points"), on every decision page, not a box every fourth.
- **The map follows the calendar.** The checkpoint map's baseline is a dated timeline of every region (a node dated "April 1940" sees Norway before the invasion, "late May 1940" sees the Low Countries fallen), not year-end snapshots, so Norway and the others now change when they did. The map never goes back in time when reports are filed out of date order, and the regions are drawn from the stop you are looking at, not from today's flags. New overrides: a nominally Vichy Free Zone, refusing Darlan, pressing Turkey into the war, the early Dnieper, and Norway's own two choices (limited landings; holding Narvik). `npm run check-map` keeps the timeline in step with the year-end table, parses every node date, and tests that those decisions move the map.
- **Advisers argue; they are not quoted.** All 570 adviser lines on the choice buttons were invented speech in real people's mouths ("I would rather win this in October with the army still intact"). Each is now what that person argued, in the third person: "Halder argues: ...". Six lines for which a real, sourced quotation exists show the real words in speech marks in place of the position (no tag, no source on the button; the source is in the register): Rommel on the first twenty-four hours, Hitler on the oil of Maikop and Grozny, Guderian on Kursk, Churchill on evacuations and on nations that go down fighting, Mussolini on Greece. `claims/quotations.json` logs each one, with a status of primary or secondary and a note saying what a fact-checker should open; five of the six are secondary (the wording is relayed by a book or a history, and still needs its primary source opened). `npm run check-quotations` (in `audit` and `test:fast`) fails on a fake quotation, an unlogged one, one whose source differs from the register, a speaker who is not the adviser shown, or any return to a `quote` field, and proves it does by running itself against deliberately bad input first.
- The outcome screen shows only the three headline changes again (the chips at the top). The movement of the meters now lives on the decision page, behind the new expand buttons (see "Meters have depth" below). The "Where you stand now" table is gone.
- Battle screens, accessibility: every button has a visible focus ring and a pressed state; the effort buttons are 44px; a chosen commander or approach carries a tick as well as a colour; the effort count beside each arm and the reserve line are announced when they change; the battle report is a live log, the front-line bar has a text alternative in words, and focus moves to a decision, the decisive hour or the counterattack when it comes up and to the verdict at the end; staff work that cannot be bought says why instead of sitting dead; small informative text is stronger; motion also follows the system's reduced-motion setting.
- Layout is mobile-first: the briefing, outcome and end-screen cards are capped at 600px (they were 672px). Phones are unchanged.
### Fixed
- **A hovered choice no longer goes black on black.** The fallback stylesheet for arbitrary-value classes was unlayered, so it beat Tailwind's hover colours (and its `sm:` sizes). It now sits in the `utilities` layer. Found while playing 1941, which had the same stylesheet.
- **A quotation and a file reference corrected.** The Guderian line on Kursk was worded differently from the one Wikipedia gives from *Panzer Leader*, and carried a page number nobody had checked; it is now the one sentence that source gives ("How many people do you think even know where Kursk is?"), without the page. The Churchill line of 28 May 1940 pointed to the wrong National Archives file (CAB 65/13); the 28 May conclusions are CAB 65/7/39 (WM (40) 144). The other four are checked for date and sense, and every note in `claims/quotations.json` now says what was and was not confirmed. `claims/glossary-and-map.json` logs the glossary and map-date facts checked.
- Saves now work outside Claude's artifact environment. The game saved your run and war record through `window.storage`, which only exists there, so on itch.io and Windows there was no Resume and no war record. The same small localStorage-backed `window.storage` that 1941 has is now in 1940.
- The Discovery Atlas listed 205 of the 250 situation reports; 45 reachable nodes were missing, so its per-campaign counts could never reach 250/250. All 250 are listed now.
### Added
- **A Holocaust section at the end of the title screen's list.** A short account, written to be read slowly, saying that the Holocaust is not a decision in the game and has been kept out of its choices on purpose. Its only named source is Yad Vashem.
- **`docs/WRITING.md`**, the writing principles for every game in the series, and `tools/check-writing.mjs`.
- **Six more Orders of Battle (31 in all).** German command: *The Kiev Pocket* (the choice at "Moscow or Kiev": drive the two panzer groups together or squeeze the ring with the infantry armies), *Manstein's Backhand Blow* (Kharkov, February and March 1943) and *Watch on the Rhine* (the Ardennes offensive, with the northern route or the centre). Soviet command: *The Kursk Belts* (hold the belts or keep the counterstroke ready), *The Strip on the Volga* (hug the Germans or let the east-bank guns do it) and *The Seelow Heights* (the tank armies on the first day, or the infantry breaking in first). Each has its own commanders, enemy setups, field decision, hard-mode order and counterattack, and a roll for the result; Kiev, Kharkov, Kursk and Stalingrad usually go the way they went; the Ardennes and Seelow can go either way, depending on your plan. The facts are logged in `claims/battles-round26.json`.
- **Eight more situation reports (278 in all).** German: *The Backhand Blow* (February-March 1943, on the path where Sixth Army was lost to the airlift: give up ground and strike the Soviet spearheads in the flank, or hold every position as Hitler ordered). Soviet, from the Dnieper to Prague, which had nothing between November 1943 and Bagration: *The Ring Is Lifted* (Leningrad, January 1944), *The Cauldron at Korsun*, *The Crimea*, *The Cannae in Romania* (Jassy-Kishinev), *The Siege of Budapest*, *The Last German Offensive* (Lake Balaton and Vienna) and *The Last Operation* (Prague). Each has a historical choice and a modeled alternative, labelled as one; the later reports and the ending read what was chosen. New dossiers: Govorov, Meretskov and Malinovsky.
- **A glossary.** In a situation report, the first mention of each term a reader may not know (Stavka, Kessel, Panzer, Lend-Lease, Typhoon, co-belligerent and the rest, 65 in all) is underlined with dots once the text has finished typing; tap or click it for a short note. The full list is under "Glossary" on the title screen, in six groups. Terms match exactly as written, so "axis of advance" is not the Axis.
- **The Eastern Front map has nine zones, not three.** The Soviet Union was North, Centre and South, "contested" from June 1941 to 1944 across its whole width, to Siberia, so one decision about one city could only flip a third of the country. It is now Leningrad & Karelia, Belorussia, Central Russia, Ukraine & Crimea, Don & Volga and the Caucasus, with the Soviet rear (Northern Russia, Volga & Urals, Central Asia) apart from them and never contested. Each front changes on its own day, dated in `MAP_TIMELINE` (Pskov, Gomel, Kharkov, Kursk, Rostov, Smolensk, Krasnodar): Belorussia and Ukraine read as German-held from the late summer and autumn of 1941, Central Russia is clear when Smolensk is retaken in September 1943, the Caucasus is contested only from July 1942 to February 1943. The 66 situation reports that highlighted the old zones were each re-read and given the zones they are about (Stalingrad is Don & Volga, Brody is Ukraine, Bagration is Belorussia). The map overrides moved with them: Moscow falling turns Central Russia, an early Dnieper clears Ukraine, and the speculative Soviet first year now shows Smolensk retaken in April 1942 clearing Central Russia, and the second break in the centre putting the front in Belorussia. `tools/split-ussr-zones.mjs` makes the nine from the three the geometry pipeline writes (the outer outlines are unchanged) and checks 56 named places against them; `npm run check-map` now also checks that every region has a shape and that the rear never changes hands. The shapes are coarse (a straight line at 46°E bounds Central Russia), and `docs/specs/eastern-front-subdivision.md` says what was built and where it is rough.
- **Five more Orders of Battle** (25 in all), one for each action added in the last rounds: **the first Italian attack on Monte Lungo** (8 December 1943), **Filottrano** (July 1944) and **the Senio at Alfonsine** (April 1945) on the co-belligerent path, and **Dubno, Lutsk and Brody** (June 1941) and **the pincers at Vyazma** (January 1942) in the Soviet campaign. Each has the usual four arms with a sheet of the real units and what happened to them, a roster of commanders tied to what they actually commanded, a pair of approaches, three hidden enemy setups (the historical one drawn twice as often), a field decision, a counterattack, and the orders from above in the campaign's hard mode. The result moves the odds of the choice the battle sits on by up to 30 points. At Monte Lungo and Brody, where history was a failure, the historical result is now the likelier outcome and the other is a speculative success the battle can earn: a first-morning victory at Monte Lungo is read in the Cassino and Adriatic reports, in the last offensive's odds and in the epilogue, and a victory at Brody counts for the later Vyazma odds as a blow that landed does. The next situation report echoes how each battle was fought. `npm run check-battle-balance` covers all 25 battles. Facts are from Wikipedia (Battle of Brody, Rzhev-Vyazma operation, Vyazma airborne operation, Italian Liberation Corps, Italian Co-belligerent Army, Spring 1945 offensive in Italy), Italian Wikipedia (Monte Lungo, Filottrano), the Liberation Route entry on Monte Lungo and the 2nd New Zealand Division's official history, checked 2026-10-08. Two sentences in the Soviet winter are corrected: the 110-kilometre ride into the German flank in January 1942 was the cavalry's, not the armies'.
- **The Allied campaign has a 1941, and its first year branches.** Seven new situation reports (270 in all) fill the gap between the Battle of Britain and Europe First, where the campaign had no Mediterranean, no Far East and no Russia; before, the first twelve decisions had a single real fork. *Tripoli or Athens* (February 1941: halt after Compass and send the best divisions to Greece, as London did, or push on to Tripoli, a gamble whose odds depend on Matériel) is a real fork: Greece leads to *The Line at Thermopylae* (evacuate, or make a stand) and *The Island Without Aircraft* (send the fleet against the Crete landings, or keep it at Alexandria); Tripoli goes straight on, and if it succeeds Libya is cleared in 1941, there is no Crusader, and the map shows it. *Four Hundred Tanks a Month* (the Moscow Conference's aid against the armour Auchinleck wants), *A Battleship for Singapore* (send Force Z, which leads to *Without Air Cover*, the sortie of December 8, or keep the ships in the Indian Ocean) and *The Relief of Tobruk* (Crusader: press on regardless, as Auchinleck did, or break off) follow. The odds of Crusader rise and fall with what was chosen before (tanks kept for Egypt, the fleet spent off Crete), and Europe First and the Pacific bill read the Far East choices. Three new endings, epilogue notes, the map, and dossiers for Wavell, O'Connor, Auchinleck, Phillips and Alan Cunningham. Facts are from Wikipedia (Operation Compass, Operation Lustre, Battle of Greece, Battle of Crete, Moscow Conference (1941), Operation Crusader, Force Z) and summaries of the Demon evacuation and the Navy's losses off Crete, checked 2026-10-07.
- **The Italian co-belligerent path fights the Germans.** Six new situation reports (263 in all), all on the co-belligerent path, from Monte Lungo to the last offensive: *The First Italian Attack* (December 1943: go in on December 8 as the Americans planned, ask for a week to rehearse, or keep the Group out), *The Adriatic Road* (June-July 1944: let the Nembo lead at Filottrano under the Polish corps, or keep the Corps on the flank), *Six Groups, and Everyone Else* (autumn 1944: accept six fully equipped Combat Groups, or press for a larger army on a lighter scale), *The Stand-Down* (November-December 1944: Alexander's proclamation and the Rome Protocols, or press the Allies to keep the partisans supplied), *Who Commands the Groups* (early 1945: attachment to Allied corps, or an Italian corps headquarters) and *The Last Offensive* (April 1945: the assault roles at the Senio and on the road to Bologna, or supporting roles). Where history did not settle the answer (whether II Corps waits, the first assault at Filottrano, whether the Allies agree to more Groups, to the drops, to an Italian corps, the spring offensive) a roll decides it, with odds that rise with what was chosen earlier, and the situation reports say what was banked. Each step is read again in the ending: four new endings, epilogue notes, the Monte Cassino and Rome situation reports, and the final report, which now says what the army, navy and air force came to (some 50,000 combat troops; nine cruisers and 33 destroyers; over 4,000 air missions). New dossiers: Dapino and Cadorna. Messe's role and fate now cover the Co-Belligerent Army's staff, and Utili's death year is corrected from 1972 to 1952. Facts are from Wikipedia (Italian Co-belligerent Army, Italian Liberation Corps, Battle of Ancona, Battle of Montecarotto, the Nembo division, Cremona, Spring 1945 offensive), the liberationroute.com page on Monte Lungo and the National WWII Museum's Italy timeline, checked 2026-10-06; figures that sources disagree on are given as ranges or in round numbers.
- **The Soviet campaign branches in its first year, and a good first year can drive the Germans back much sooner.** Seven new situation reports (257 in all). Four are real decisions the campaign had left out and are on every path: *Six Mechanized Corps* (June 1941: throw all six corps in as Directive No. 3 orders, wait two days and concentrate them, or break contact and keep the armour), *The Salient at Yelnya* (September 1941: clear it, as Zhukov did, or build the Vyazma line), *Everywhere at Once* (January 1942, after the December counteroffensive: Stalin's general offensive, Zhukov's concentration on the Western axis, or a pause) and *The Barvenkovo Salient* (May 1942: Timoshenko's offensive, as launched, or cancel it and leave the salient). The other three are speculation, labelled as such, and are reached only on the concentrated path: *The Pincers at Vyazma*, *Smolensk, Before the Thaw* and *Mars, Without the Salient*. What the player banked earlier raises the odds of the Vyazma ring (the armour kept in June, the Southwestern Front's survivors kept in October, a December blow that landed, Yelnya) and the situation reports say so. If the ring closes, the Rzhev salient never exists and the Rzhev summer offensive is skipped; Smolensk can be retaken in April 1942 (seventeen months before it was); an autumn offensive in the centre can break it a second time; the projected end of the war comes one or two months earlier; the map shows the centre cleared; Uranus has better odds; and there are six new endings. Giving up the Izyum salient instead of attacking from it also helps Uranus. Choices that disobey Moscow cost suspicion in NKVD Mode (cancelling Kharkov can get the command recalled). The historical figures are from Wikipedia (Battle of Brody, Yelnya offensive, Rzhev-Vyazma operation, Second Battle of Kharkov), checked 2026-10-06; new advisers Kirponos and Shaposhnikov have dossiers.
- **Save and leave the field.** The Order of Battle planning screen and the battle report each have a button that saves the game with the battle in it: the plan so far, the hidden enemy setup, the intelligence already bought and the readings already given, so loading cannot redraw anything. Resume puts the player back on the planning screen exactly as it was, or on the report, which starts again from its first line with the orders as given. Committing a plan saves it at once, and closing the page mid-battle writes the same save. The menu's War in Progress card says which battle it is. It is not offered after the verdict, when the roll has been made. A damaged battle section falls back to the briefing. Covered by `npm run test:battle-resume` (in `test:fast`).
- **Battle sounds**, heard only with the Sound setting on (off until asked for): a soft click as effort goes in and out, a radio burst for each report and for the staff's readings, distant guns as a battle opens and when a beat swings hard against you, paper on the map table for the exercise, two notes for the verdict (rising on a win, sinking on a loss) and a soft note for each way the meters moved. None carries information the screen does not also give in words.
- Four early-war Order of Battle screens, one more for each campaign: **the Meuse crossing at Sedan** (German, May 1940), **the Moscow counteroffensive** (Soviet, December 1941), **Battle of Britain Day** (Allied, 15 September 1940) and **the Little St Bernard** (Italian Alps offensive, June 1940). Each puts a roll behind a decision that was a fixed outcome before (the Manstein Plan, the winter counteroffensive, Park's doctrine, the push in the Alps), so the historical result is now the likelier outcome and no longer the certain one. The first outcome of each roll is the text that used to be the choice's own, so the historical line reads the same when the player does well. The Alps are the exception: history there was the unlucky result, so the fortunate outcome is the minority and comes first. The next situation report mentions how the battle went.
- Order of Battle improvements, for all 14 battles: a short "How an Order of Battle works" guide (open the first time, closed afterwards); "Spread effort evenly" and "Clear all effort" buttons; a one-sentence "Your plan so far" summary before the commit button; a "Ground and weather" line for each battle, with matching modifiers on the arms it affects; and a **field decision** in the middle of every battle, written from the real alternatives of that battle (the turn to Prokhorovka, the destroyers at Omaha, the covering force on PQ-17, the road or the high ground in the Alps, and so on). The best answer to a field decision depends on the enemy's hidden setup, so reading the intelligence matters, and some answers cost Manpower, Matériel or Initiative.
- Battle-specific twists: **two-phase battles** (Battle of Britain Day, PQ-17, Second Schweinfurt) have a second hidden enemy setup that is revealed half way through, and the plan is weighed against both; **attrition** (frostbite in the Alps, the cold before Moscow, exposure on Monte Marrone) costs Manpower when the assault is committed too heavily, and the screen says so beforehand; **defensive** battles (Battle of Britain Day) count the enemy's counterattack for half as much again.
- After every battle the report adds a line from military intelligence: whether your orders improved or cost us chance of victory, in points to the nearest five (the change your plan made, never the odds themselves).
- The War Record has a **Battle Record**: each battle you have fought, how many times, how many won, the best result, how many of its enemy setups you have met, and last time's commander and field decision. Battles not yet fought show as blanks. It is recorded when a war ends, like the rest of the record.
- `check-battle-balance` now also checks every field decision (no option may be best under every enemy setup, every option within its limits) and runs the two-phase battles against every pair of enemy setups.
- **Effort, not chits.** The pool a player spreads across a battle's arms is now "effort" (doctrinally, the weight of effort behind the main effort), in points: "Effort in reserve: 2 of 6", "Add effort to Air Attack", "Effort put into Armour carries further under Hoth". Nothing about the rules changed.
- **Matériel, not Fuel.** The second meter is now called Matériel on every screen (men and matériel); its internal key is unchanged, so saves keep working. It stands for fuel, ammunition, steel, shipping and rail together. Under it on the briefing screen sit four readings in words (Fuel & Oil, Ammunition, Armour & Steel, Shipping & Rail: Short, Strained, Adequate or Plentiful) which show which strand your decisions have been feeding or starving, and the outcome screen says which strand a Matériel change fell on. They explain the one number and do not replace it. A choice is filed to a strand by what its text is about, or by an explicit matStrand on the choice; `npm run check-materiel-strands` shows how, and is part of `npm run audit`.
- The four new battles' favourable outcomes now keep the choice's original Manpower, Matériel and Initiative impact (they had carried zero).
- **Six more Order of Battle screens**, taking the game to twenty: **Operation Uranus** (Soviet, November 1942), **Operation Dynamo**, the Dunkirk evacuation (Allied, May 1940), **the Pindus winter** (Italian, the reserve in Albania, winter 1940-41), **Operation Mercury**, the invasion of Crete (German, May 1941), **the relief of Bastogne** (Allied, December 1944) and **Cape Matapan** (Italian, March 1941). Each is on a decision that carried a roll or a fixed outcome before, with the historical outcome the first and likelier one (Matapan is the exception, where history was the unlucky result). Crete, Bastogne and Matapan are fought in two phases.
- **Let your staff plan it.** Every battle has a button that hands the whole Order of Battle to the staff: a balanced plan with no favourite commander, made without sight of the enemy's setup, and a battle report that runs itself with the best average answer to each field decision. It costs nothing, and a player who reads the enemy does better (`check-battle-balance` now prints the average bonus of a careless plan, the staff's plan and an informed player for every battle, and fails if the staff plan is worse than careless play or comes within two points of an informed player). A tick-box, "Always let my staff plan battles", does it every time, and the battle report offers a way back.
- **Map exercise.** For one Initiative the staff war-game the plan on the map table against two enemy setups drawn at random and say how it held against each. It cannot say which one is real. It sits beside the Reconnaissance Pass (which tells you about the enemy) and the Staff Assessment (which judges the plan against what the enemy really has).
- **Order-of-battle sheets.** Under every arm of every battle, a sheet naming the real units and strengths, and what actually happened to them, written as history to read and never as advice.
- **Strands bite.** An arm that draws on a Matériel strand (artillery on Ammunition, armour on Armour & Steel, aircraft on Fuel & Oil, supply on Shipping & Rail) carries more weight when the strand reads Plentiful and less when it reads Strained or Short, and the planning screen says so beside the arm.
- **Hard modes in battle.** In a campaign's hard mode each battle carries orders from above: a locked approach (Führer Mode, Coalition Mode, Axis Mode), a locked or unavailable commander, or an order that the line may not give ground (NKVD Mode). They are modeled, and the screen states them before you plan.
- **A playtest kit.** The end screen has "A note for the author", one line with the campaign, mode, ending and every battle's grade, enemy setup, commander, field decision and whether the staff planned it, to paste into a comment on the game's page; `docs/PLAYTEST.md` says how to run a test. Round 24: the author's note itself is gone from the end screen; the After-Action Summary carries the same facts in plain words (rank, ending, standing, battles with their setups, commanders and decisions, advisers, objectives), and is what a tester should paste.
- `check:orphans` in the fast tests (all 250 nodes reachable, including the hard-mode-only ones).
- Save-compatibility test (`npm run test:saves`) and a save-migration helper (`NODE_ALIASES`, `SAVE_MIGRATIONS`, `migrateSave`, docs/SAVES.md).
